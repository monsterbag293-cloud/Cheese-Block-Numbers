🧀 Cheese Block Numbers Taxonomy

The Complete Architectural Rulebook

Based on the Fondue Melt Test, Meta-Ranks, Additive Recipes, Trinity Sums, and Cheese Resonance

🌟 The Cheese Kingdom (Melts to 1)

  • 1. Cheesy Numbers: Any number that reduces to 1 through iterative applications of (L × S) + (L - S) (or d² for 1 digit).
  • 2. Super Cheese Numbers: A Cheesy number whose 1-based position rank among all Cheesy numbers is also Cheesy (e.g. 8 is Cheesy #4; 4 is Cheesy).
  • 3. Pure Cheese Numbers: A Super Cheese number formed by adding two smaller Super Cheese numbers (P = A + B, e.g. 26 = 24 + 2).

🧫 The Decay Kingdom (Loops Forever)

  • 1. Moldy Numbers: Any number that fails to reach 1 and gets trapped in one of the 3 canonical flavor loops:
    • Gouda: 9 ↔ 81 ↔ 15
    • Brie: 13 ↔ 5 ↔ 25
    • Cheddar: 7 ↔ 49 ↔ 41
  • 2. Blue Cheese Numbers: A Moldy number whose 1-based rank among all Moldy numbers is also Moldy (e.g. 12 is Moldy #6; 6 is Moldy).
  • 3. Extra Stinky Numbers: A Blue Cheese number formed by adding two smaller Blue Cheese numbers (S = A + B, e.g. 12 = 6 + 6).

⚡ Trinity Sums & Crossover Defectors

Cheesy Boi Formula:

CB(x) = Cheesy(x) + Super(x) + Pure(x)

If CB(x) stays in cheese, it is a Pure Cheesy Boi. If it spoils into decay, it receives the title of Happy Moldy / Happy Blue / Happy Extra Stinky Number!

Rotten Formula:

R(x) = Moldy(x) + Blue(x) + Stinky(x)

If R(x) stays in decay, it is a Rotten Number. If it miraculously melts to 1, it defects and earns a Sad Cheesy title (including the ultra-rare Sad Cheesy Boi)!

🧀 Cheese Resonance Numbers

Score

R(n) = Σ C(fᵏ(n)) × (k+1)², k = 0 … t−1

Each Cheesy value in the path adds the square of its one-based position. The terminal 1 is excluded. Path uses last digit × sum of preceding digits.

Primary classes (by R)

0 No Resonance · 1–10 Mildly · 11–30 Aged · 31–60 Strong · 61+ Extreme

Density (by Q, the Cheesy count)

1 Single · 2 Double · 3 Triple · 4+ Dense

Positional subclasses

Early, Late, Consecutive, Scattered, Full-Spectrum, and Midpoint. A number can hold several at once.

Perfect Resonance

n is perfect if C(R(n)) = 1

Perfect numbers can also be Double, Triple, Extreme, or Pure Perfect.

Worked example, n = 26: 26 → 16 → 11 → 1. All three values are Cheesy, so R = 1² + 2² + 3² = 14 (Aged), Q = 3 (Triple), with Early, Late, Consecutive, Full-Spectrum, and Midpoint subclasses. 14 is not Cheesy, so 26 is not Perfect.

🧀 Rule: Cheese Resonance Numbers

Cheese Resonance Calculator

Scores the transformation path of n by where its Cheesy values fall along it.

This rule uses its own transform: L is the last digit and S is the sum of the preceding digits. The Analyzer and Rulebook use the largest and smallest digits instead. "Cheesy" follows the Engine's existing definition.

Rule Summary

Score

R(n) = Σ C(fᵏ(n)) × (k+1)², k = 0 … t−1

Each Cheesy value in the path adds the square of its one-based position. The terminal 1 is excluded.

Primary classes

0: No Resonance · 1–10: Mildly · 11–30: Aged · 31–60: Strong · 61+: Extreme

Density (Q = Cheesy count)

Q = 1 Single · 2 Double · 3 Triple · 4 or more Dense

Positional subclasses

Early (start Cheesy) · Late (last value before 1 Cheesy) · Consecutive · Scattered · Full-Spectrum · Midpoint (a Cheesy position within 1 of t/2)

Perfect Resonance

PerfectResonance(n) = C(R(n))

If R(n) is itself Cheesy, n is a Perfect Resonance Number. It can then also be Double (Q ≥ 2), Triple (Q ≥ 3), Extreme (R ≥ 61), or Pure (all Cheesy values in the path are distinct).

Rule Summary

Rotations

Every distinct cyclic rotation of the digits, with leading zeros removed. 123 gives {123, 231, 312}.

Score W(n)

W(n) = Cheesy rotations / total rotations

0 Rusty · below 0.5 Wobbly · 0.5 Balanced · above 0.5 Greasy · 1 Perfect Cheese Wheel

Special subclasses

Golden (all rotations Cheesy and Super) · Moldy (all Moldy) · Mixed (Cheesy and Moldy rotations) · Broken (exactly one Cheesy) · Infinite (all rotations identical) · Reverse (n and its reversal Cheesy) · Double-Decker (two or more Super) · Wheel of Misfortune (Super but no Cheesy).

Dominance

D(n) = 2·Cheesy + 3·Super + 5·Pure (counted over rotations)

0 Wheel of Shame · 1–4 Cheese Apprentice · 5–9 Wheel Knight · 10–19 Cheese Overlord · 20+ Supreme Wheel Emperor

🌌 Transcendental Numbers & Theoretical Caveats

Critical mathematical analysis of π, e, and compound expressions like tan(π·e)

⚠️ 1. The Representation Ambiguity

An Identity Fingerprint currently depends on the algebraic expression chosen rather than the real number itself. For example, writing φ = (1+√5)/2 yields fingerprint 6, but using the nested radical identity φ = √(1+φ) yields different rational floor approximations. A rigorous theory requires a canonical representation invariant.

❓ 2. Unproven Status of tan(π·e)

While $\pi$ and $e$ are proven transcendental, the rationality and algebraic status of compound expressions like $\pi + e$, $\pi \cdot e$, and $ an(\pi e)$ remain unsolved open problems in mathematics. The fact that decimal digits appear non-terminating on a calculator is not proof of irrationality.

💡 3. The Path to Mathematical Rigor

To make fingerprints truly intrinsic to each real number $lpha \in \mathbb{R}$, we propose adopting Simple Continued Fractions: $lpha = [a_0; a_1, a_2, \dots]$. Because simple continued fractions are unique and representation-independent, taking the sum of the first $k$ partial quotients provides an identity-free fingerprint!
